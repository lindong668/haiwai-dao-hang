Free Photoshop Alternatives That Run in Your Browser (2026)

Updated Oct 20267 min readComparison guide

Photoshop is excellent and expensive, and for most people most of the time it is also more than the job requires. If your task is cropping, resizing, retouching a blemish, removing a background or preparing an image for the web, a free browser editor will do it — often faster, because there is nothing to install.

What You Give Up And What You Do Not

Being honest about the trade-off saves disappointment. Browser editors do not replace Photoshop for:

  • Complex multi-layer compositing over long sessions
  • Advanced 3D, video timelines or RAW processing pipelines
  • CMYK print production with colour management
  • Very large files measured in hundreds of megabytes

They do handle, comfortably:

  • Layered editing, masks and adjustment layers
  • Retouching, cropping, resizing and colour correction
  • Background removal and compositing
  • Export to web formats, including PSD in some cases

For a large majority of everyday image work, the free option is genuinely sufficient.

Photopea

Photopea is the closest thing to Photoshop in a browser tab. The layout, the shortcuts and the tool behaviour are deliberately familiar: layers panel on the right, toolbar on the left, and Ctrl+J, Ctrl+T and the rest working as your hands expect.

Its standout capability is format support. It opens PSD, XD, Sketch, Figma, AI, EPS, PDF and RAW files, so a design file received from a colleague can be opened on a machine with no design software at all. Files process locally, and there is no watermark.

The compromises are interface ads around the edges and weaker performance on very large files.

Piccalc

PicCalc takes a different approach: rather than one deep editor, it offers 24 focused tools — background removal, ID photo creation, resizing, conversion, compression, filters, watermarking, EXIF removal, QR generation and more.

Everything is processed locally in the browser, so images never leave your device. For anyone working with ID documents, contracts, medical images or client artwork, that is a decisive advantage over cloud editors. There is no quota and no watermark.

It is not a layered compositing tool, but for the "I need to do one specific thing to this image" case it is usually faster than opening a full editor.

Canva

Canva is a design platform rather than a photo editor, and that distinction matters. If you are producing a social graphic, presentation slide, poster or resume, its templates and asset library save enormous time.

If you are editing a photograph — retouching, colour correcting, cutting out a subject — it is the wrong tool. Use a dedicated editor, then import the finished image into Canva for layout.

Gimp

GIMP deserves a mention as the option for people who want a real desktop application with no subscription. It is genuinely powerful, fully offline and free.

Its cost is the interface, which does not follow Photoshop conventions and requires real adjustment. Choose it if you need offline heavy editing and are willing to learn it; choose a browser tool if you want results today.

How To Choose

Need real layered editing?

Use Photopea. Closest to Photoshop, no cost, no install.

Need one specific job done fast, or privacy?

Use PicCalc. 24 focused tools, local processing, no upload.

Making a social graphic or slide?

Use Canva. Templates beat blank canvases.

Offline and powerful, willing to learn?

Use GIMP.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best free alternative to Photoshop?

For layered editing in a browser, Photopea — it closely mirrors Photoshop's interface, shortcuts and PSD support. For quick single-purpose jobs and privacy, PicCalc is faster.

Can I open PSD files without Photoshop?

Yes. Photopea opens PSD with layers, masks and text intact, and can re-export as PSD.

Is there a free editor that does not upload my photos?

Yes. Both Photopea and PicCalc process images locally in the browser, so files never leave your device.

Do I need to install anything?

No. Photopea, PicCalc and Canva all run in a browser tab. GIMP requires installation but works fully offline.

Can these handle professional print work?

Not reliably. CMYK colour management and large-format output are where desktop Photoshop still matters.

Are free editors watermarked?

Photopea and PicCalc do not watermark output. Some tools place features behind a paid plan rather than marking your file.
