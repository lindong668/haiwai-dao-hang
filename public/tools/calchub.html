What It Is — Free Finance Calculators That Show Their Formulas

CalcHub is a single-page suite of nine financial calculators covering the questions people actually ask before signing something: what will my monthly payment be, what is this credit card really costing me, how long until the balance is gone, and can I afford this at all. Rather than spreading those across five bookmarked sites with different interfaces, they sit in one page and share the same input conventions.

The design leans towards transparency. Each result is accompanied by the formula that produced it and the assumptions behind it — compounding frequency, whether tax and insurance are included, how APR is derived from the nominal rate. That is a meaningful difference from the black-box widgets embedded on many lender and comparison sites.

It is free, requires no account, and runs entirely client-side. You can open it, type in a salary figure, and close the tab knowing that number never left your device.

How To Work Out What House You Can Afford (Step By Step)

Pick a calculator from the top navigation, fill in the fields, and read the result together with the formula printed underneath. Change one input at a time — raise the down payment, shorten the term, add a monthly prepayment — and watch how the output moves. That comparison loop is where the tool earns its keep.

TipOn a phone, the calculators stack vertically and the formula block sits directly under each result, so you do not have to scroll between the two.

Why Seeing The Formula Matters When You Compare Loans

Two calculators can disagree by hundreds of dollars a month and both be arithmetically correct, simply because one compounds monthly and the other semi-annually, or because one folds insurance into the payment. If you cannot see the assumptions, you cannot tell which is right for you. CalcHub prints them, which turns an opaque number into something you can check against your lender's own disclosure.

Limitations

The mortgage figure covers principal and interest only. Real monthly outlay usually adds property tax, home insurance, and private mortgage insurance when the down payment is below 20% — so your lender's number will very likely be higher. The currency converter uses mid-market reference rates, which is not the rate a bank or card network gives you; expect a margin of roughly one to three percent at the point of exchange.

Educational use onlyThese calculators show what the arithmetic produces under the assumptions you enter. They are not financial, legal or tax advice, and no tool here tells you what to borrow or which product to choose. Always confirm against your lender's official disclosure before committing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CalcHub free to use?

Yes. All nine calculators are free, with no registration, no email capture and no daily-use cap. You can run as many scenarios as you like.

Does it store the numbers I type in?

No. Every calculation happens in your browser. Loan amounts, interest rates and balances stay in the page and are discarded when you close the tab — nothing is sent to a server.

Where do the currency rates come from?

Rates come from a public exchange-rate API and refresh daily. They are mid-market reference rates, not the rate your bank or card issuer will give you, which usually includes a margin.

Why does my bank's monthly payment differ from the result?

The calculator covers principal and interest. Real payments usually also include property tax, home insurance, and mortgage insurance if your down payment is under 20%. Add those separately to match your lender's figure.

What is the difference between APR and the interest rate?

The interest rate is what you are charged on the balance. APR also folds in certain fees and expresses the total yearly cost as a single percentage, which makes different offers easier to compare. The APR tool shows how the two diverge.

Is this financial advice?

No. CalcHub is an educational calculation tool. It shows you what the arithmetic produces under the assumptions you enter; it does not tell you what to borrow, what to buy, or which product to choose. Check any decision against your lender's official disclosure.

How much house can I afford on my salary?

Open the Affordability Calculator and enter your annual income, expected down payment, interest rate and term. It works backwards from a monthly payment you can comfortably sustain to the loan size that implies. Bear in mind the result covers principal and interest only — add property tax, home insurance and any association fee to reach the real monthly figure.

How long will it take to pay off my credit card if I only make the minimum payment?

Enter your balance, APR and minimum payment into the Credit Card Payoff calculator. It returns the number of months and the total interest you would pay along the way. Most people find the figure sobering — switching to a fixed higher monthly payment usually cuts both dramatically, and the tool lays the two side by side.

Should I make extra mortgage payments or invest the money instead?

Run both scenarios in the Prepayment Calculator: it shows how much interest a recurring extra payment saves and how many months it removes from the term. Compare that guaranteed saving against what the same money might earn elsewhere. The tool does the arithmetic; the decision rests on your rate, your tax position and your appetite for risk.

Is a 15-year or a 30-year mortgage better?

Change the term in the Mortgage Calculator and compare the two. The 15-year loan carries a higher monthly payment but a far lower total interest bill; the 30-year frees up monthly cash flow for everything else. The tool shows both the payment and the lifetime interest, so you can judge which trade-off fits your situation.

How do I convert USD to CNY at the mid-market rate?

Open the Currency Converter and enter the amount. It uses rates refreshed daily from a public source and shows the mid-market reference rate — the rate banks use among themselves. Your bank or card issuer will normally apply a margin on top, so treat the result as a benchmark rather than the price you will actually be given.

Can I use these calculators on my phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to mobile browsers, so you can run a mortgage or payoff scenario on a phone. Because nothing is uploaded, the calculators also keep working on a slow or metered connection once the page has loaded.
